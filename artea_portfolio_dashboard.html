Decision summary

The page follows the order of a decision: the question, the evidence, the uncertainty, and the action it supports.

What is being improved

Revenue growth from coupon promotions. The decision is whether to send a 20% coupon broadly, and whether any customer group deserves a targeted follow-up test.

How success is judged

Recorded revenue per assigned customer, with non-buyers counted at zero. Purchase rate and transactions per customer are secondary: they describe how revenue moved, not whether it moved.

What the data shows

Revenue per customer was without a coupon and with one, a difference of (95% CI to , p = ). The interval includes both a loss and a gain.

What I recommend

Do not treat a broad coupon send as a proven revenue strategy. Confirm how discounts are recorded, then test cart abandoners in a new randomized experiment with the effect size and sample set in advance.

The experiment does not establish a revenue benefit from a broad coupon rollout. Cart abandoners are a candidate for further testing, not a validated target segment.

The revenue interval is wide enough to include both a loss and a gain per customer, so the data confirms neither. It is not a finding that the coupon failed.

05

Does the answer change under profit assumptions?

AssumptionUncertainty

Recorded revenue is not profit, and the data does not say whether revenue is recorded before or after the coupon discount. I model both cases. The outputs are scenarios driven by the inputs below. They are not observed profit, and no coupon redemption rate was observed.

Scenario A: recorded revenue is after the discount

Both groups earn the same contribution margin on net revenue.

Incremental contribution = margin × (revenue per customer difference) − contact cost per coupon customer

Scenario B: recorded revenue is before the discount

Every coupon-group transaction gets a 20% discount. Cost of goods is proportional to pre-discount revenue.

Incremental contribution = (margin − 0.20) × coupon revenue per customer − margin × control revenue per customer − contact cost

Margins in A and B are measured on different bases (net versus pre-discount revenue), so the same percentage is not the same economics. The intervals reflect sampling variation given the assumptions and exclude uncertainty in the assumptions themselves. The 20% discount comes from the case lecture as cited in the project report.

Question
Does offering a 20% coupon to customers who abandoned a cart increase net revenue per eligible customer?
Population
Customers who left items in a cart. are available in the next-campaign file.
Arms and assignment
No coupon (control) versus 20% coupon, randomized 1:1 at the customer level, with duplicate enrollment prevented and other promotions kept comparable.
Primary metric
Net revenue per assigned customer after discounts and refunds, including customers who buy nothing.
Secondary metrics
Purchase rate and transactions per customer.
Guardrails
Contribution per customer, refunds, unsubscribes, and later full-price purchasing. The current data does not contain all of these.
Observation window
28 days after assignment for every customer. This is a new design choice. It is not the confirmed window of the original experiment.
Analysis plan
Two-sided significance level of 5% and 80% power, with a fixed sample and no stopping when a preliminary p-value crosses the threshold.
Decision rule
Set the minimum economically meaningful effect before launch, then judge the primary metric together with contribution and the guardrails.

How large must the experiment be?

The required sample depends on the smallest effect worth detecting. It grows with the inverse square of that effect: halve the target and the sample quadruples.

What the relationship means for planning

Two experiments, two questions

An all-customer experiment asks whether a broad send raises revenue per customer. A cart-only experiment asks whether the coupon raises revenue among cart abandoners. The answer to one does not substitute for the other, so the population should follow the business question. Recruitment time cannot be estimated without traffic data.

Higher purchase activity did not translate into a confirmed revenue gain.

The available cart abandoners can detect about per customer at 80% power. Detecting needs customers in total, times the number available.

Evidence: sample-size analysis

3 recommendations

1

Do not recommend a broad coupon send as a proven revenue-growth strategy.

The primary metric does not support it, and the all-customer contribution estimate is negative in every Scenario A and B combination tested.

Linked to insights 1 and 2. Evidence: experiment result, profit scenarios
2

Confirm how discounts are recorded, the outcome window, and the cost definitions.

For the cart-abandoner group, Scenario A gives a positive estimate in margin and cost combinations while Scenario B is negative in . Whether recorded revenue is before or after the discount decides the sign.

Linked to insight 1. Evidence: profit scenarios, open definitions
3

Treat cart abandoners as a hypothesis for a new randomized test, with the effect size and sample fixed in advance.

Agree the smallest economically meaningful effect from margin, contact cost, and discount before launch, size the test from it, and report group sizes, uncertainty, and profit assumptions next to the headline metric. Do not multiply the observed lift by and call it incremental revenue.

Methods and tools

SQLite ran five SQL files for data-quality checks, the KPI metric tree, segment performance, the next-campaign profile, and segment sufficient statistics. Python with pandas, NumPy, and SciPy computed the inference. This page is plain HTML and JavaScript. BigQuery and Tableau were not used.
